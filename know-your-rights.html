“Know Your rights”

Interaction design - Civic & legal / June 2026

Tags: Analysing Psychology Information design Intervention design Storytelling & narrative

Duration: 5 weeks

Norway has some of the strongest labor laws in the world. Most of the workers they're supposed to protect have never read them — or don't know how to use them.

School is a rare space to push boundaries, test assumptions, and fail. Sopra Steria challenged students to develop their own concept for a digital product within a short timeframe, building a rock-solid rationale for every decision, and holding both a business and a design perspective throughout. What mattered wasn't the size of the problem, but the ability to define a focused problem space, explain why it's worth solving, and translate it into a realistic solution.

During this project I found an untapped market space with huge opportunities — one that could change the way society works.

00 Challenge & my role

Challenge

The brief was open: develop a concept for a digital product in five weeks. It could start from a small everyday frustration or a larger systemic issue — size didn't matter. What mattered was defining a focused scope, explaining why it was worth solving, and turning it into something real and testable — while holding both a business and a design perspective throughout the project.

My role

I framed my own project. I pitched three directions: a better way to report to the police, a stronger embassy service, and workers' rights — and chose to go deeper on workers' rights.

My role was to deliver the required outcomes — an interactive prototype, a value reflection, and a value proposition canvas — build a future plan, and meet with Sopra Steria along the way for feedback and course correction. I also made sure I was present for every class excursion.
